Cozy picture books are topping the 2026 holiday gift lists

Gift-guide season has arrived, and this year the children's lists lean cozy. The Associated Press holiday roundup mixes snug board books with a few delightfully spooky reads, and one title that keeps coming up is Your Truck by Jon Klassen, published by Candlewick Press. Klassen's dry humour and quiet pictures have made him a favourite with parents who read the same book forty nights in a row.

November also brought a strong crop of new releases. Book Riot's monthly roundup highlights Good Bear, Bad Bear by Hyewon Yum, out November 3: a little girl decides that if her sister is always the good one, she must be the bad one. It is a gentle doorway into a feeling many siblings know well. And for early readers who love comics, the newest Dog Man by Dav Pilkey is the most anticipated children's book of the month.

Award season is underway too. The New York Times and New York Public Library have named their ten Best Illustrated Children's Books of 2026, a list worth browsing if you want a book that will also look beautiful on the shelf.

How to choose a picture book gift

  • Ages 0–2: sturdy board books with bold pictures, rhythm and repetition.
  • Ages 3–5: a story with a clear feeling at its heart (fear of the dark, a new sibling, sharing) and pictures that reward a second look.
  • Ages 6–8: longer read-alouds, funny early chapter books and comics that build confidence.
  • Any age: think about what the child already loves (trucks, animals, space) and start there.
Try this tonight

Write a short message on the first page before you wrap it. A dated inscription turns a book into a keepsake a child will find again years later.
