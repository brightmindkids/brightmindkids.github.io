Do audiobooks count as reading? What new research tells parents

Many parents wonder whether a story in headphones is 'real' reading. The latest research from the UK's National Literacy Trust offers a reassuring answer: for many children, listening is one of the ways into books, not a replacement for them.

The charity's 2026 audio report, based on a survey of more than 110,000 children and young people aged 8 to 18, found that almost half now listen to audiobooks, podcasts or both. More than a quarter said that listening makes them more interested in reading books, and around three in ten audiobook listeners said audio helps them enjoy stories when reading on the page feels hard.

Children also told researchers that listening helps them relax, feel happy and feel more confident. A separate report published in October 2026 looked at families with children under five and found that audio stories are already a normal part of everyday life for many of them, with parents pointing to benefits for language, learning and wellbeing.

Why listening helps

  • It builds vocabulary and background knowledge, which children later need to understand what they read.
  • It lets children enjoy stories that are above their current reading level.
  • It models expressive reading: pauses, voices and feeling.
  • It can be a lifeline for children who find decoding tiring or who have dyslexia.

Making audio work alongside books

  • Pair the audiobook with the printed book so your child can follow the words.
  • Use audio in 'dead time': car journeys, tidying up, waiting rooms.
  • Talk about the story afterwards, just as you would after a read-aloud.
  • Keep shared read-alouds too. Your voice and your lap are still the best format for little ones.

Researchers are clear that audio is not meant to replace learning to read. Children still need daily practice with print. But if a story in headphones gets a reluctant reader excited about books, that is a win.

Try this tonight

Next time your child finishes an audiobook, borrow the paper version and let them find their favourite scene in the book. It turns listening into reading in a natural way.
